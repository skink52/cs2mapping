## What was built

`tools/navradar.py` (new, about 290 lines): `read_nav` parses a CS2 `.nav` (v30-36) up to the ladders: places, polygons (shared
corner list, v31+), corner gravity (skipped), movable meshes (v35+), the v36 binary KV3 blocks (skipped by their v5 header sizes,
stored or compressed; a compressed block with binary blobs is refused), areas (id, flags, hull, corners, connections per edge,
hiding spots skipped, ladders above/below), ladders (v33+ has the two extra bottom areas). Generation params and the custom-data
KV3 after the ladders are not read (a radar does not need them). `load` takes a compiled map `.vpk` (`maps/<name>.nav` inside,
read-only through `tools/vpk.py`) or a loose `.nav`. `render`: areas of one hull (default the hull with most areas), optional
`--min-island` drops connected groups (by the nav connections) smaller than N areas, each area grown by `--expand` units (default
16, the agent radius; Hammer5Tools' NavMeshOffset idea; convex mitred offset per area), framed exactly like `radar.run` (0.5
percentile trim, span x 1.06 + 256, square, `scale = span / size`), shaded per pixel from each area's least-squares height plane with
radar.py's untextured floor colours (2nd-98th percentile of corner heights), dark outline at the walkable edge and at height
steps > 40 units (radar.py's wall threshold), ladders as orange lines. Overview text in radar.py's format; spawn fractions 0.5
unless `--ct X,Y` / `--t X,Y` are given (the nav has no spawns).

Parsed every nav on the PC (91: 85 v36, 6 v35, addons and stock maps), 0 errors; 64 of 64 addon navs and all stock ones.
Render time 1-1.5 s per map at 1024. Checked: **unit tested** (5 tests, synthetic v30/31/33/35/36 navs built in the test,
`tools/tests/test_navradar.py`); real runs below are a look at the png only. Not compiled, not in-game verified.

## Real-map comparison (work\borrowed_test\navradar\, `<map>_nav.png` vs `<map>_floor.png` = radar.run untextured on the vmap)

| Map | navradar pos / scale | radar.py pos / scale | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| de_dust2 (stock, sanity) | -2555 3294 / 4.59 | (Valve's: -2476 3239 / 4.4) | Recognisable dust2; framing within 4% of Valve's own overview |
| mg_cs16 | -1753 2168 / 10.02 | -3313 3917 / 11.74 | The map holds three sub-maps; the nav covers only two (office-like block and the bottom one). The dust-style block top-left has **no nav areas**, so it is missing from the nav radar. Where both draw, the nav outline is cleaner (no stray roof/edge faces), but props and wall thickness show as holes/gaps rather than outlines. |
| mg_trenchwarfare | -2501 2988 / 4.87 | -7183 7183 / 14.03 | radar.py takes in the whole outer terrain (scenery you can't reach), so the playable trenches are a small patch in the middle. The nav radar frames only the walkable part: trenches 3x larger on screen, the north field with crater holes, the central low box. **Nav is clearly better here.** |
| mg_nanite_temple | -922 921 / 1.80 | -7267 5910 / 14.27 | The nav covers only the showdown arena (268 areas, one island); the 12 vaults, hub and corridor rooms have no nav. radar.py shows every room. **Nav is worse here** (rooms reached by teleport get no nav when they're not seeded). |

Summary: the nav radar shows exactly where players can walk and frames tightly on it, which beats radar.py when the map has big
unreachable scenery (terrain, skybox-ish floors) and is cleaner around roofs. It loses every room without nav (teleport-only rooms,
areas the generator didn't reach) and has no materials, water or wall tops. It needs a compiled map with a nav, and the imported
ports compile empty navs (every ba_jail port: 834 bytes, 0 areas), so for ports it only helps after a nav is generated.

pipeline.md, Radar paragraph:

    **Radar from the nav mesh (option).** `tools/navradar.py <map.vpk> <out.png> --overview <out.txt>` draws the radar from the compiled
    map's nav mesh instead of its floors: only where a player can walk, framed tightly on it (same pos/scale rules as `radar`, so the png

    and copy the png/txt into the addon. Unit tested.
